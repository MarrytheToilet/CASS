**1. Batched end-to-end cost.** The original 48 sequences are six batch-8 calls. On RTX 5090/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (BF16), extraction takes 0.183–0.216 s across three predeclared tasks. Collecting only the required layers and caching the solver Gram matrix reduces full task adaptation from 0.978–3.105 s to 0.276–0.369 s; all 447 query/batch comparisons retain identical generations. These are warm-model/dictionary measurements. Optimized 48-sequence adaptation uses 83.9–105.7 GPU-board joules (original: 179.9–374.7 J).

The contextual variant below uses 28 sequences in two calls and takes 0.165–0.411 s / 50.4–76.5 GPU-board joules to adapt. With a common greedy engine and reusable-prefix caching for both methods, batch-25 CASS/ICL4 serving takes 183–186/184–191 ms under the eight-token budget. Answer-delimited decoding gives task-dependent ratios of 0.826–1.031x. Thus the paper's 10.2x figure concerns amortized input tokens against ten-shot prompting; it is not a measured latency speedup. We provide batch1/8/25 timings, energy, setup and numerical-parity checks separately. For multiple retained task states, operator-plus-prefix tensor accounting gives median paired ICL4/CASS memory ratios of 6.5x on entity extraction and 8.6x on classification (three tasks each), excluding shared model/dictionary memory.

Across all seven broader NLP tasks, with a 32-token limit and first-answer stopping, batch-8 CASS/ICL4 throughput ratios range from 0.281 to 1.217. The accompanying task-level table includes energy, setup and parsed-answer checks; cached serving costs depend on the task and generated answer lengths.

A 320-cell oracle factorial on 20 non-development known tasks isolates an intervention-setting effect: at native prompting/gain2, layer24 gives 0.263 versus 0.042 for layers14+20 (+22.1 points [12.2, 33.1]).
