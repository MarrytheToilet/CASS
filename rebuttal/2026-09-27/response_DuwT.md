Response to Reviewer DuwT

**1. Contribution and relation to existing methods.** Encoding demonstrations into an activation representation and injecting it at inference have established precedents. [Hendel et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.624/) study task-vector compression, and [ICV](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liu24bx.html) derives latent steering vectors from demonstrations. We do not claim the first encoding/decoding scheme or a new sparse-coding algorithm. CASS contributes a specific diagnosis and use of cross-task activation structure: removing the leading shared component exposes reusable task-family structure, and a sparse selection of skill subspaces supplies a corrective operator for a task absent from the dictionary. On the eight-task ablation, removing that component gives 0.54 accuracy versus 0.32 without removal and 0.33 after random rank-one removal. The principal-component controls distinguish this effect from arbitrary projection.

The closest comparisons isolate task-vector extraction, retrieval, subspace correction, and routing. Our ICV-style and nearest-skill controls are matched implementations of those mechanisms; they should not be described as full reproductions of every published system. A new fixed-routing comparison holds replacement outputs and the three shared seeds constant: CASS reaches 0.4575 versus 0.4210 for z-only on LOTO, a conditional +3.65-point difference with 95% CI [+0.65, +7.27].

**2. Clearer module organization.** We will structure the method around three inputs and outputs. Offline mining takes contrastive activations and returns a de-shared subspace and anchor for each known task. Few-shot coding takes the new task's demonstrations and returns z, selected support S, and residual epsilon. Serving injects z together with a gated correction defined by S. A separate routing rule chooses prompt-state replacement for weak signals; residual-based ICL escalation is an optional cost–quality setting, absent from the main-table full-method results. This presentation makes the sparse solver's role and the actual injected direction explicit.

**3. Mathematical details.** Proposition 1 needs a weighted KKT argument. Write q_t=sqrt(r_t), A_S for the true-support blocks, G=A_S^T A_S, W=diag(q_t I), and P=A_S G^{-1}A_S^T. At the restricted optimum, the residual is r=(I-P)e+lambda A_S G^{-1}Wv, with block norms ||v_t||<=1. Define theta as the maximum weighted off-support norm of the second operator over such v, and eta=max_{j outside S} ||U_j^T(I-P)e||/q_j. If G is invertible, theta<1, and lambda>eta/(1-theta), every inactive-block KKT inequality is strict. The global solution then has no false positives. Its coefficient error is bounded by epsilon/sigma_min(A_S) + lambda sqrt(sum q_t^2)/sigma_min(A_S)^2; an O(epsilon) conclusion also needs lambda=O(epsilon). We will provide the full proof and correct the empirical interpretation: maximum coherence is approximately 0.99, so the original bound certifies only one active block. Proposition 2 requires a smooth score and a Taylor-remainder bound; it does not certify greedy exact-match execution of the multi-step system. These are conditional analyses, rather than new general sparse-recovery theory.

**4. Cross-model comparison and dictionary size.** The task registry, input–output datasets, and deterministic split procedure are shared across models. Each model's dictionary is extracted in its own activation coordinates. No model parameters are trained using the dictionary. The dictionary-versus-z-only comparison is made within each model on the same task protocol; cross-model runs assess portability across the tested models. Their pretraining corpora are not controlled, so those runs do not isolate architecture as a causal variable.

The existing extended scaling experiment uses six held-out tasks, five dictionary draws, and two demonstration seeds. Dictionary sizes 5/10/20/30/40/46 give mean accuracies 0.437/0.491/0.540/0.545/0.526/0.519. The pool contains 47 tasks, with the target excluded. These results show improvement over small dictionaries followed by saturation, rather than strict monotonic growth. We will report the complete curve with that interpretation. Scaling to hundreds of distinct skills remains outside the measured range.
